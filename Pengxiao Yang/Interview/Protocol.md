# Interview protocol

Team 17 · The Weekly Split  
Interviewer: Pengxiao Yang  
Source questions: `interview questions(1).docx`

Use this script as-is. Do not lead with the prototype. If they ask what we are building, wait until all five questions are done.

## Intro (say this)

We are looking at how sharehouses split grocery and takeaway bills. There are five questions. Talk about a real week if you can. I will write notes. You will show up in the repo as a code (P07, P08, …), not by name. You can skip anything.

## Question 1 — Swallowed resentment (anonymous veto)

Tell me about a specific time when a shared house bill (like a grocery run or group takeout) included items you didn't consume or barely used, but you ended up paying an equal share anyway. Why did you choose not to speak up at the time, and how did that decision affect the dynamic in the house afterward?

- Social/mobile focus: face-to-face confrontation avoidance, social pressure, communication barriers.
- What we are testing: psychological safety, individual fairness, need for anonymous mediation.

## Question 2 — Settlement ritual (sync vs async)

Walk me through the exact routine your house currently uses to calculate and request money for shared expenses. Do you ever sit in the same room when the math is being done and the requests are sent, or does it happen entirely over text/push notifications? Where does that current routine feel the most awkward or stressful?

- Social/mobile focus: async push vs colocated interaction, domestic routines.
- What we are testing: transparency, anxiety from unexpected digital debt requests.

## Question 3 — Unequal share (fairness slider / blind voting)

Think of a time when your house bought something to share (like a bulk grocery order, a large takeout meal, or shared drinks) but people consumed vastly different amounts. How did the group decide whether to split the cost evenly or proportionally, and how did you navigate the conversation if someone felt they were paying for more than they actually consumed?

- Social/mobile focus: negotiating shared value, real-time consensus, subjective consumption.
- What we are testing: equity, freerider resentment, subjective fairness.

## Question 4 — Non-monetary trades (bounty / chore barter)

Can you describe a situation where someone in the house was short on cash for a shared expense, so they compensated by taking on extra household duties (like buying the next round, doing a deep clean, or cooking)? How did the house negotiate and agree on the value of that favor?

- Social/mobile focus: alternative value, informal domestic economies, group consensus.
- What we are testing: empathy, domestic equity, financial flexibility.

## Question 5 — Diffusing tension (gamification / app as the bad guy)

Describe a time when a tense conversation about household money, chores, or responsibilities was successfully diffused by humor, a quick game (like rock-paper-scissors or a coin flip), or an external mediator. How did shifting the focus away from a direct argument change the mood in the room?

- Social/mobile focus: social comfort, tension relief, mediated decision-making.
- What we are testing: fun, preserving friendships, removing personal blame.

## After the five questions (optional, only if they want to see the product)

Show The Weekly Split. Do not ask “would you use this?” first. Ask: which move (claim, veto, slider, bounty, spinner) matches something that already happens in your house, and which one would feel fake.
