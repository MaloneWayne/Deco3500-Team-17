# Participant 02

**Participant ID:** C02  
**Living situation:** University accommodation with four housemates  
**Interview type:** Semi-structured interview  
**Duration:** Approximately 10 minutes

## Q1. Paying despite feeling a split was unfair

**Interviewer:**  
Can you remember a shared expense where you paid an equal amount even though you felt that was not completely fair?

**Participant:**  
Takeaway is probably the most common example. Sometimes everyone orders together but one person orders something more expensive, or some people share sides that I do not eat. Usually somebody just divides the final total evenly because it is easier.

**Interviewer:**  
Would you normally challenge it?

**Participant:**  
Only if the difference was quite large. If I only lose three or four dollars, I normally do not say anything.

**Interviewer:**  
Why?

**Participant:**  
Because the social cost feels bigger than the money. You do not want people to think you are counting every dollar.

---

## Q2. Current settlement routine

**Interviewer:**  
How do you currently clear shared debts?

**Participant:**  
Mostly by bank transfer and Messenger. Everyone pays at different times.

**Interviewer:**  
How would you feel about everyone spending ten minutes together once a week to settle shared expenses?

**Participant:**  
I think it could actually be easier, as long as it stays quick. The annoying part now is getting small payment requests at random times during the week.

**Interviewer:**  
Would being in the same room make it more awkward?

**Participant:**  
If we had to verbally negotiate every item, probably yes. But if everyone is sitting together while using their own phones, that feels different because you still have some privacy.

---

## Q3. Proportional sharing

**Interviewer:**  
Suppose four people bought a shared item but used very different amounts. How should it be divided?

**Participant:**  
I do not think equal splitting is always fair, but exact measurement is unrealistic too.

**Interviewer:**  
Would an approximate percentage or slider work?

**Participant:**  
Probably. You could just indicate whether you used a little or a lot. I would not expect it to be scientifically accurate. It would just give everyone a way to express their own view.

**Interviewer:**  
Should the system automatically correct the values if they do not total 100 percent?

**Participant:**  
If they are slightly over, maybe the system can normalise it. But if there is part of the cost that nobody is claiming, then I think the group needs to look at it again.

---

## Q4. Chore barter

**Interviewer:**  
Would you accept someone doing an extra chore instead of paying a small shared debt?

**Participant:**  
Probably not very often. Money and chores could become another argument because people value chores differently. I think it is interesting, but it should probably be optional rather than part of the main settlement process.

---

## Q5. Anonymous review

**Interviewer:**  
Would anonymous review make you more comfortable raising a problem?

**Participant:**  
For expensive items, yes. But I would not want people to anonymously reject the same item over and over again.

**Interviewer:**  
How would you prevent that?

**Participant:**  
Maybe the app should ask why someone is reviewing it, or limit how many times the same item can go back into review.

---

## Key Quotes

> “The social cost feels bigger than the money.”

> “If everyone is sitting together while using their own phones, that feels different because you still have some privacy.”

## Initial Insight

Co-location does not necessarily require public confrontation. A shared settlement session combined with private mobile input may preserve group coordination while reducing interpersonal pressure.
