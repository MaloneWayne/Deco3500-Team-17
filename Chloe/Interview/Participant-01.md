# Participant 01

**Participant ID:** C01  
**Living situation:** Shared house with three other university students  
**Interview type:** Semi-structured interview  
**Duration:** Approximately 10–12 minutes

## Q1. Paying for something you did not use

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a time when a shared house bill included something you did not consume or barely used, but you still paid an equal share.

**Participant:**  
This happens quite often with groceries. Sometimes one person buys snacks, soft drinks and other things together with the basic groceries. I do not really eat many of the snacks, but normally the whole receipt is divided equally between everyone.

**Interviewer:**  
Why did you choose not to say anything?

**Participant:**  
If the difference is only a few dollars, I do not want to sound too serious about money. I feel like if I question every small item it could make living together uncomfortable, so normally I just pay.

**Interviewer:**  
Did that affect how you felt afterwards?

**Participant:**  
Not enough to start an argument, but if it happens repeatedly it becomes annoying. You start feeling like you are always paying slightly more than you should.

---

## Q2. How the house currently settles expenses

**Interviewer:**  
Walk me through how your household normally calculates and requests money for shared expenses.

**Participant:**  
Usually one person pays first and then sends a photo of the receipt or a message in the group chat. Sometimes they calculate it immediately and sometimes it happens a few days later.

**Interviewer:**  
Do you normally sit together when this happens?

**Participant:**  
No. Almost everything happens through messages.

**Interviewer:**  
Where does that process feel awkward?

**Participant:**  
Sometimes you suddenly get a message saying you owe forty or fifty dollars and you do not really remember what was included. If you ask questions afterwards, it can feel like you are challenging the person who calculated it.

---

## Q3. Unequal consumption

**Interviewer:**  
What happens if everyone shared something but consumed very different amounts?

**Participant:**  
Normally we still split it evenly because trying to calculate exactly how much everyone ate feels too difficult.

**Interviewer:**  
Would you be comfortable privately indicating roughly how much you consumed?

**Participant:**  
Yes. I think that would be easier if everyone did it separately. I would not want to say directly that someone ate twice as much as me, but I would be comfortable entering something like twenty or thirty percent on my phone.

**Interviewer:**  
What if everyone's estimates do not total exactly 100 percent?

**Participant:**  
Then I think the app should show that the item needs to be reviewed rather than deciding automatically.

---

## Q4. Chores instead of money

**Interviewer:**  
Would your household ever exchange household duties for a small debt?

**Participant:**  
Maybe for a small amount. For example, if somebody owed five or ten dollars, doing the kitchen cleaning or taking out the bins could work. But everyone should agree because different people value chores differently.

---

## Q5. Reducing tension

**Interviewer:**  
Would having a digital system manage disagreements make the conversation easier?

**Participant:**  
Yes, especially if nobody has to personally accuse someone else. If the system simply says that an item needs review, it feels less personal.

**Interviewer:**  
Would anonymity help?

**Participant:**  
For more expensive or disputed items, yes. I think people would be more willing to raise a concern if they did not immediately have to explain themselves in front of everyone.

---

## Key Quotes

> “I would not want to say directly that someone ate twice as much as me, but I would be comfortable entering something like twenty or thirty percent on my phone.”

> “If the system simply says that an item needs review, it feels less personal.”

## Initial Insight

The participant accepted small unfair costs in order to avoid creating tension with housemates. Private input may provide a lower-pressure way to express perceived consumption without directly accusing another person.
