# Prototype Interview Transcript 4

## Feature 1: The Receipt Draft

**Interviewer:** Looking at this screen without me explaining it, what do you think is happening here, and what would you do next?

**Participant:** I think the app is showing an item from the receipt and asking if I used it. I’d choose Claim or Pass.

**Interviewer:** If you saw an item you only took a tiny bite of, would you tap Claim or Pass? Why?

**Participant:** I might claim it if I actually had some, but only if the cost gets split fairly.

**Interviewer:** How would you feel seeing that 3 of your housemates claimed an item, but you didn't? Would you feel pressured to claim it?

**Participant:** Not too much. I’d notice it, but I wouldn’t claim something just because everyone else did.

**Interviewer:** What happens if two people genuinely disagree on whether an item was shared? Does swiping bypass the conversation, or just postpone it?

**Participant:** It probably just postpones it. Swiping is easy, but a real disagreement still needs a conversation.

## Feature 2: The Icebreaker Veto & Review Phase

**Interviewer:** What do you think the Veto Tokens are for? What would make you press Play Veto Card instead of Looks Fair?

**Participant:** The veto seems like a way to pause the bill if you think something is wrong. I’d use it for a bigger issue.

**Interviewer:** Your screen turns red and this countdown starts. What do you imagine the room feels like right now?

**Participant:** A bit tense. The red screen makes it feel more serious than it probably is.

**Interviewer:** How do you think the voting and the slider resolve the issue?

**Participant:** The vote gives the group a decision, and the slider makes the split less all-or-nothing.

**Interviewer:** The veto trigger is anonymous, but if there are only 3 or 4 people in the room, can people easily guess who called it? Would that create tension?

**Participant:** Yes, especially with only three people. You could probably work out who used it.

**Interviewer:** Does the 2-minute Table Talk timer help you break the ice, or does it feel confrontational?

**Participant:** I’d prefer no timer. I understand why it’s there, but it could make people rush.

**Interviewer:** If one person's item gets voted as Personal by everyone else, how do you think they would feel? Does blind voting feel fair?

**Participant:** They might feel ganged up on. The result can be fair and still feel uncomfortable.

**Interviewer:** On the pizza slider, do you trust your housemates to be honest? What stops someone from always picking Just a Nibble to save money?

**Participant:** I’d mostly trust them, but there’s definitely room to cheat a little.

## Feature 3: The Barter & Penalty System

**Interviewer:** What is Housemate B offering here, and what is your immediate reaction to voting on this?

**Participant:** They want to cover part of the debt by doing housework. I think it’s a decent option.

**Interviewer:** Would you personally accept household chores in exchange for paying real money out of your pocket? Why or why not?

**Participant:** Yes, if the chore is useful and the amount is small.

**Interviewer:** How would you decide if taking out bins for 2 weeks is worth $10? What happens if they don't do the chore after the debt is cleared?

**Participant:** We’d agree on a rough value. If they don’t do the chore, they should still owe the money.

**Interviewer:** Would proposing this make someone feel embarrassed about their financial situation, or does it feel empowering?

**Participant:** It depends on the person. Some might like having another option, but others could feel embarrassed.

## Feature 4: The Orphan Item Penalty Game

**Interviewer:** What do you think happened to cause this screen to appear? How do you feel about the wheel deciding?

**Participant:** Nobody claimed the item, so the wheel chooses who gets stuck with it. I don’t think that’s fair.

**Interviewer:** For essential communal items like dishwasher pods or toilet paper, does turning payment into a roulette wheel feel funny, or does it feel unfair if a broke student loses?

**Participant:** For shared basics, I really don’t like it. Someone with less money could randomly get the worst outcome.

**Interviewer:** Can you imagine a situation where someone deliberately refuses to claim an item just to gamble on the wheel?

**Participant:** Yes. If people know they might avoid paying, some of them probably will.

**Interviewer:** Would this resolve an argument, or cause an argument right at the table?

**Participant:** It would probably create more tension, not solve it.

**Interviewer:** So overall, would you want this feature in the app?

**Participant:** No. I’d remove this feature and just split essential shared items automatically.
