# User Research: Interview Findings

## Research Goal

The purpose of the interviews was to understand how university students living with housemates manage shared expenses, how they experience unfair cost splitting, and how social relationships influence financial decisions.

The interviews focused on five main areas:

1. Experiences of paying for items they did not use
2. Current shared expense routines
3. Unequal consumption and proportional splitting
4. Non-monetary contributions such as chores
5. Ways to reduce tension during financial discussions

---

## Participants

Six participants with shared-living experience were interviewed.

Participants included domestic and international university students living with friends, partners, or other housemates.

---

# Key Findings

## Finding 1: Fairness vs. Social Comfort

Many participants said they sometimes accept an unfair split because they do not want to create tension with their housemates.

Participants described situations where they paid for food, groceries, or household items that they did not fully use.

### Key Insight

People may know that a split is unfair, but speaking up can feel socially risky.

### Design Implication

The system should provide a low-pressure way for users to raise concerns without directly blaming another person.

---

## Finding 2: Current Tools Support Payment, Not Negotiation

Participants commonly use group chats, bank transfers, WeChat, Messenger, or Splitwise to manage shared expenses.

These tools are useful for recording payments, but they do not help users decide whether an item should be shared, personal, or split proportionally.

### Key Insight

Existing tools help manage debt, but they do not support negotiation about fairness.

### Design Implication

Weekly Split should focus on the decision-making process before payment, including claiming items, reviewing costs, and agreeing on the final split.

---

## Finding 3: Equal Splitting Is Easy but Not Always Fair

Participants often use equal splitting because it is simple and convenient.

However, problems occur when one person consumes much more than others or when some people do not use an item at all.

### Key Insight

There is a tension between convenience and fairness.

### Design Implication

The system should support different splitting methods, including equal split, item-based split, personal items, and proportional split.

---

## Finding 4: Fairness Is Subjective

Participants described situations where it was difficult to decide what a fair amount should be.

For example, people may consume different amounts of shared food or use household products differently.

### Key Insight

Fairness is often negotiated rather than calculated.

### Design Implication

Features such as group voting or proportional input could help housemates express different views and reach a shared decision.

---

## Finding 5: Anonymous Mediation Can Reduce Pressure

Several participants said that a neutral system or anonymous voting could make difficult financial conversations easier.

They felt that using an external system could reduce personal blame.

### Key Insight

Anonymous interaction may reduce confrontation, but users still need to understand how the final decision was made.

### Design Implication

The Anonymous Veto feature should protect user identity while keeping the review process transparent.

---

## Finding 6: Chores Can Be an Alternative Contribution

Some participants described situations where housemates used cooking, cleaning, or other tasks to balance shared responsibilities.

However, they were uncomfortable assigning an exact financial value to each chore.

### Key Insight

Non-monetary contributions can feel fair, but fixed financial values may create new disagreements.

### Design Implication

Users should be able to propose a trade, while other housemates vote to accept or reject it.

---

## Finding 7: Gamification Can Reduce Tension

Participants mentioned humor, rock-paper-scissors, jokes, and random decision tools as ways to reduce tension.

These approaches helped make difficult conversations feel less personal.

### Key Insight

Light gamification can reduce blame and social tension.

### Design Implication

Gamification should support the conversation without making serious financial concerns feel unimportant.

---

# Main User Needs

- A safe way to disagree
- Clear explanation of how costs are calculated
- Flexible ways to split expenses
- Shared awareness of who has reviewed or paid
- Less awkward confrontation
- Control over personal and shared expenses

---

# Overall Research Insight

The main problem is not calculating shared expenses. It is negotiating fairness without damaging housemate relationships.
