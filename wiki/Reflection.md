# Reflection

This page is the group looking at the work, not at the user.

## What worked

We narrowed. Campus-wide thrift pins were a plausible studio idea and a weak local product. The kitchen is a real room in St Lucia. You can point at it.

We built. The mid-fi prototype runs the full ritual. That is more honest than a Figma file that never has to do the maths.

We let the brief bite. “Do not target university students” stopped us from shipping another student-only campus toy. Housemates are the users. The suburb is the bound.

## Gaps

**Field evidence is thin.** We have frameworks, a working prototype, and lived knowledge of sharehouses. We do not yet have a Sunday session with a house that is not us. The grading criteria ask whether the problem is supported by strong evidence. This is the weakest plank.

**Five people.** The brief wants four. We listed everyone. Approval still matters.

**Pages.** The live GitHub link depends on the repo owner turning on GitHub Pages. Until then, teammates run `npm run dev`.

**Receipt capture.** The demo uses a seeded Woolies-like list. A real house will want to type or scan. We did not build that, on purpose. Social mechanics first.

## Did we understand the problem well enough?

Well enough to prototype. Not well enough to stop testing. We know the speech act is the pain. We do not yet know how often a real house will veto, or whether they will laugh at the spinner and then resent it.

## Were the methods effective?

The wheel and the six dimensions were effective at killing ideas. Prototyping was effective at making the veto feel like a product, not a paragraph. Interviews would be the missing method, and they should happen in the kitchen, not in a lab.

## What we would do differently

Start in one real St Lucia house in week two. Photograph one receipt (with consent). Play the ritual on paper before code. Code only the moves that survived that night.

And write less about “smart campus” sooner.
