# Related and Future Problems

Wicked problems do not sit still. This kitchen fight is a symptom. The prototype would also make new ones.

## What this problem sits on

**Housing cost.** St Lucia and Indooroopilly rents are high. Four strangers share a lease because one bedroom each is the only way the numbers work. Money talk is sharp because the house is already tight.

**Invisible labour.** Someone always becomes the default shopper and the default accountant. That person is often the one who cares enough to start the spreadsheet. Our problem is downstream of that unpaid admin.

**Power in the house.** The person who paid the $150 grocery bill holds the receipt. The person who is new to the lease speaks last. A “fair split” can hide that.

**Culture of niceness.** Australian sharehouses prize being easy to live with. The social cost of “that’s yours, not ours” is higher than the dollar amount.

So: we are not curing housing. We are treating one weekly flare-up that housing produces.

## What the solution could break next

**Majority as truth.** If three people tap “personal” on the protein, the fourth is stuck. The tribe has spoken. That can bully a housemate who actually bought a shared item.

**Veto as a weapon.** An anonymous pause is a gift to a shy person. It is also a gift to someone who wants to stall every bill.

**Gamification of debt.** Spinners and sliders are funny until someone is actually broke. A joke wheel that assigns $14.50 can feel cruel.

**The accountant is still there.** Drew still floated the groceries in our demo. The ritual splits the bill. It does not stop one person from being the bank.

**Exclusion.** No phone, no English, no Sunday night at home: you miss the ritual and inherit the result.

**Data.** A house that logs every item is a house that can later weaponise “you always claim the snacks.”

We should watch those in testing, not wait for a complaint after launch.
