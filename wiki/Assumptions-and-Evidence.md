# Assumptions and Evidence

## Starting assumptions

We began with four bets.

1. **People will flag an unfair item if they do not have to put their name on it.** Motivation: we have all watched someone eat the cost of a housemate’s specialty food rather than start a scene.
2. **A short, same-room ritual beats an async chase for money.** Motivation: Splitwise already exists. People still hate the push notification. Being together changes the social physics.
3. **Chores can stand in for cash.** Motivation: houses already trade labour informally (“I’ll do bins if you skip my share”). The app should make that trade visible, not pretend money is the only currency.
4. **The awkwardness is the product problem.** Motivation: the maths is easy. The conversation is not.

## Why we held them

These are lived-house assumptions, not survey results. Sharehouse kitchens in St Lucia are close. You hear the fridge. You see who bought the fancy cheese. The cost of speaking up is social, not arithmetic.

We also assumed that if the **app** pauses the bill and asks “communal or personal?”, nobody has to play detective.

## What shifted

Early studio work pointed at a broader “smart campus budgeting” story: pins on cheap eats, ambient thrift presence, cooling-off alerts for online shopping.

That story treated the campus as the stage. Once we sat with the Social Possibility Wheel and the six social dimensions, the sharpest tension was smaller and closer: **four people, one receipt, one table**.

The delta is real. We started with public, location-based sharing. We ended with a **colocated, anonymous, game-like split**. The local community stayed the same (these suburbs). The unit of design shrank from campus to household.

## Evidence we have

- Critique of current tools (Splitwise, spreadsheets, “just Venmo me”). They solve tracking. They do not mediate the conversation.
- A working mid-fi prototype that lets a house walk the full ritual: claim, orphan, veto, review, bounty, lock.
- Course frameworks (Location / Social Possibility Wheel) used as a filter, not as decoration.

## Evidence we still need

We have not run a proper field probe in a St Lucia kitchen yet. That is a gap. The next honest test is a real Sunday night: four housemates, one phone each, one receipt, ten minutes. Count how often someone uses the veto. Count whether anyone asks “who pressed it?”
