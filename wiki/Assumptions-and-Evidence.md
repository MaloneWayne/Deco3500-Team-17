# Assumptions and Evidence

## Starting assumptions

We began with four bets.

1. **People will flag an unfair item if they do not have to put their name on it.** Motivation: we have all watched someone eat the cost of a housemate’s specialty food rather than start a scene.
2. **A short, same-room ritual beats an async chase for money.** Motivation: Splitwise already exists. People still hate the ping that arrives later. Being together changes the social physics.
3. **Chores can stand in for cash.** Motivation: houses already trade labour informally (“I’ll do bins if you skip my share”).
4. **The awkwardness is the product problem.** Motivation: the maths is easy. The conversation is not.

## Why we held them

These started as lived-house bets, not as survey results. Sharehouse kitchens in St Lucia, Indooroopilly, and Toowong are close. You hear the fridge. You see who bought the fancy cheese. The cost of speaking up is social, not arithmetic.

We also assumed that if the **app** pauses the bill and asks “communal or personal?”, nobody has to play detective.

## What the interviews did to those bets

Rize Ma ran six semi-structured interviews (P01–P06) with people who currently split household costs in sharehouses. Full notes: [`Ruize Ma/Interview Transcript 01.docx`](https://github.com/MaloneWayne/Deco3500-Team-17/blob/main/Ruize%20Ma/Interview%20Transcript%2001.docx).

Some of them study. That is a demographic fact of inner-west Brisbane housing, not our user definition. We asked about the lease, the fridge, and the group chat. We did not ask about campus life.

| Assumption | What we heard | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Anonymity lowers the cost of objecting | P06 wanted anonymous voting. P05 said it would feel different if *the app* asked for a review, instead of a housemate. P01 stayed quiet over snacks rather than “look too serious about a few dollars.” | **Held.** Strongest confirmed bet. |
| 2. Same-room beats async | P01: “We never sit together to calculate it.” P02 gets a Messenger demand for $30 they cannot place. P03’s numbers arrive as WeChat screenshots and someone else’s maths. P06 already likes a Sunday-night rhythm. | **Held, with a tweak.** The weekly cadence is wanted. Sitting together is rare. That gap is the design. |
| 3. Chores can replace cash | Informal trades exist (P01, P02, P05). P03 and P04 pushed back: putting a dollar value on cleaning would start a *second* fight. | **Softened.** Bounty cards stay optional. The house must accept the trade. We do not price chores in the background. |
| 4. Awkwardness, not arithmetic | P04: Splitwise records payments; “the difficult part happens before entering the expense.” P03: people accept small unfairness because exact calculation is also annoying. | **Held.** This is now the problem statement, not a hunch. |

The delta that matters: we started by treating chores as a cute equivalent to money. The interviews said **peace beats perfect fairness**, and that **pricing labour is a new argument**. The product shifted from “pay with chores” to “propose a trade, let the others vote.”

We also dropped the early campus story (thrift pins, ambient save-badges, checkout cooling-off). Those ideas aimed at students-as-students. The interviews kept pointing at the receipt.

## Evidence we have

- Six interviews. Recurring pattern: equal split by default, silence unless the difference is huge, tools that log money but do not negotiate “shared vs personal.”
- Critique of Splitwise, group-chat screenshots, and PayID. Matches what P01–P06 already do.
- A playable mid-fi ritual plus visual flows in [`Ruize Ma/`](https://github.com/MaloneWayne/Deco3500-Team-17/tree/main/Ruize%20Ma).

## Evidence we still need

The interviews are recall, not a live Sunday. We have not yet sat in a St Lucia kitchen with a real Woolies receipt and watched whether anyone asks “who pressed veto?” That is the next test, not a claim we can make today.
