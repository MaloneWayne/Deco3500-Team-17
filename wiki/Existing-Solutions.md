# Existing Solutions

## How houses split now

**One person and a spreadsheet.** Fast for the accountant. Humiliating for everyone else. The house inherits that person’s idea of “fair.”

**Splitwise and similar apps.** They log debts. They ping you later. The notification is cold. It arrives when you are not together. That is when money talk feels like a demand, not a ritual.

**Cash on the fridge.** Honest. Easy to lose. Useless for a $22 powder and an $80 pizza in the same week.

**“Just send it on PayID.”** The transfer is easy. The argument about *who owes what* still happened in the hallway.

**Do nothing and seethe.** Common. Cheap. Corrosive.

## Why these fail the actual problem

They all assume the hard part is **remembering the number**. It is not.

The hard part is saying, in front of someone you live with, that their protein powder is not a household staple. Current tools leave that speech act to the human. Then they only store the result.

They also train an expectation: money talk is **async, individual, and slightly shameful**. Open the app alone. Get a red badge. Pay. Close it.

A good solution for this kitchen would invert that. Same room. Short. The system asks the rude question so the housemate does not have to.

## What this tells us about expectations

People already know how to swipe, vote, and split a bill. They have seen Tinder, Seen, and Splitwise. They will expect:

- item-level control, not only a total
- a final “who pays whom”
- something they can finish in one sitting

They will not expect a lecture about financial literacy. They will bounce if the house has to nominate a treasurer.
