# The Problem

**In St Lucia sharehouses, the weekly shop still gets split by one person doing maths in the kitchen, so housemates swallow unfair costs rather than say “I didn’t eat that.”**

## Where this plays out

This is a **local household** problem, not a campus-student app.

St Lucia, Indooroopilly, and Toowong are packed with sharehouses. Four people. One fridge. One Coles or Woolies run on Sunday. Someone pays on their card. Then the house has to settle.

The fight is rarely about the $8 milk. It is about the $22 protein powder that one person bought, the pizza someone barely touched, and the cleaning spray nobody wants to claim. Saying that out loud, at the table, feels like accusing a housemate of stealing.

So people stay quiet. They pay. They keep score in their head. The house gets colder.

## What we are not doing

The brief asked us not to treat “university students” as the user group. We are not.

Our users are **housemates who live together in this stretch of Brisbane**. Some study. Some work. The thing they share is a lease and a kitchen, not a student ID.

A location-based campus thrift map would have aimed at students as students. We walked away from that. The heat is at the kitchen table.

## Why it matters here

These suburbs sit next to UQ, but the housing is ordinary rental stock. Kitchens are small. People are in and out. Sunday is often the only night everyone is home.

If the split is ugly, it does not stay in an app. It sits in the house for the rest of the week. That is the local shape of the problem.
