# Solution Classes

The same kitchen problem can be attacked in several mediums. We compared them instead of jumping to “make an app.”

## House rules and posters

A laminated “fair split” sheet on the fridge.

- Strength: zero tech, always visible.
- Weakness: rules do not speak when the protein powder appears. They also cannot hide who complained.

## Physical objects

A bowl of tokens. A spinner. A notepad on the table.

- Strength: already a ritual. Fits a Sunday night.
- Weakness: no memory, no anonymity, easy to cheat, painful with 15 receipt lines.

## Ambient / awareness displays

A shared screen that shows who is “in a save week.”

- Strength: low talk.
- Weakness: vague. Does not close a $150 grocery bill.

## Async money apps

Splitwise, spreadsheets, bank splits.

- Strength: they already work for tracking.
- Weakness: they export the fight to later, when nobody is in the same mood.

## Colocated interactive ritual (what we built)

Phones on the table. Same items at the same time. Swipe, veto, review, lock.

- Strength: uses the fact that the house is already together. Keeps input private. Makes the app the bad guy.
- Weakness: needs everyone home. Needs a phone each. Can feel like a game when someone is genuinely broke.

## Why this class wins here

St Lucia sharehouses already have the colocation. Sunday night already exists. The missing piece is a **mediator**, not a ledger.

A poster cannot run a blind vote. Splitwise will not sit at the table. A spinner is funny for one orphan item (P01 already does rock-paper-scissors) and useless for a full receipt.

So: a mobile web ritual, no install, playable in a browser, tied to a household rather than a campus map.
