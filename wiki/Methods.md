# Methods

HCI methods have to match the claim. Our claim is social, not statistical: **the split is painful because of face, not because people cannot add**.

## What we used

**Framework analysis.** We ran the six social dimensions on the current direction: objective, mechanism, technology, people’s contribution, consequences, key assumption. That stopped us from pitching “an app that tracks spending” and forced us to say what social move the system makes.

**Social Possibility Wheel.** We diverged on *shared objects* and *awareness* before we converged. Shared objects became a household pool and a bounty card. Awareness became the live claim list on everyone else’s phone, without forcing talk.

**Concept matrix.** We wrote three concepts in the same four-colour split (overview, mechanism, contribution, consequences):

- Campus Thrift Radar (location)
- Budget Quest (shared objects)
- Cooling-off Buddy Alert (contextual push)

We kept the one that could be **played in a room**.

**Prototyping as inquiry.** We built a mid-fi web ritual instead of another slide. Building the swipe, the veto flash, and the fairness slider showed us where the design actually carries the conflict. That is data. It is not interview data, but it is not nothing.

## What the work revealed

The campus pin-drop idea needed constant mobility and photo uploads. It also needed students-as-students. That fights the brief and fights the kitchen.

Budget Quest was closer. Gamified shared funds still assume people will log expenses alone. Our housemates already sit together. Use that.

The cooling-off buddy is a good impulse-buy idea. It is not a weekly house split.

The nuance: **anonymity has to stay on the phone, while the bodies stay in the room.** If we send them away to “talk it out,” the product has failed.

## What we did not do (yet)

No large questionnaire. No diary study. No pretend quotes from fictional housemates. Those would look like evidence and would not be.

When we test, the method should be a **colocated session** with a real receipt, plus a short interview after about whether anyone felt they had to speak. That matches the claim.
